nice to meet you! I am

Avery Fernandez

If I am not designing or coding, you will probably find me reading high-stakes fantasy books or crocheting.

I’m a UI/UX designer and developer who uses my scientific, artistic, and interpersonal skills to build intuitive, accessible, and visually appealing websites and apps.

I completed my Bachelor’s in Informatics at the University of California, Irvine and my Master’s in Human-Computer Interaction at the Rochester Institute of Technology, where my research explored accessibility through noise-sensitivity management and the use of mainstream technologies as assistive tools for the d/DHH community.

I work end to end: from user research and heuristic evaluation to shipping full-stack builds in Next.js and React. My experience as both a researcher and a developer has given me insights that have made me a better designer. I can empathize with users’ pain points and translate them into requirements, while also designing things that others can easily build with existing frameworks.

PACM HCI (MobileHCI ’25) · 2025

Informing the Design of Mobile and Wearable Technology for Noise Sensitivity

Through eight participatory design workshops with people who experience noise sensitivity and their carers, we explored how mobile and wearable technology could support their sensory well-being. We found these tools can build awareness of sensory triggers and enable both self- and collaborative regulation, and we surface three tensions for designing them.

Emani Hicks, Sohyeon Park, Avery Mavrovounioti, et al.

ASSETS ’24 · 2024

Accessibility through Awareness of Noise Sensitivity Management and Regulation Practices

In a three-month interview study with 21 people who experience noise sensitivity and 9 caregivers, we found that awareness is the key factor shaping how they manage and regulate it — from avoidance and sound masking to coping strategies like journaling and setting boundaries. We introduce “joint awareness” to foster collaborative management and offer design principles for technology that supports noise-sensitivity awareness, management, and regulation.

Emani Hicks, Avery Mavrovounioti, et al.

IDC ’23 · 2023

Supporting Noise Sensitivity and Emotion Regulation with Children

Noise-sensitive children can struggle to regulate their emotions around loud or uncontrollable sounds, so we ran co-design sessions to explore an assistive app that supports their self-regulation. This work-in-progress shares early findings from two pilot co-design sessions and considerations for co-designing with noise-sensitive children.

Emani Dotch, Jialuo Hu, Avery Mavrovounioti, et al.
